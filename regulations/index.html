01 / LEGAL BASIS

Regulations.

Two documents govern exam coordination: the EER (what gets graded, when, and who decides) and the Rules of Procedure (what happens inside the exam room on the day).

02 / CORE

Core articles.

10 entries

Art. 5.2Grading

Course examination grades

Grades are on a 1–10 scale to one decimal. The student needs 5.5 without rounding to pass. No participation = NG (fail). For resits, the highest grade is the final grade.

In practice This is the legal source of the '5.45–5.49 rounds DOWN to fail' rule — it's literally 'without rounding'. Also why you fill NG when a student didn't show up.
Art. 5.8Grading

Determination & announcement of results

The examiner determines the result within 10 working days and provides ESD with the information needed to announce it to the student.

In practice This is the formal CC grading deadline you chase every period.
Art. 5.9Students & accommodations

Right of inspection

Students may inspect their evaluated work within 10 working days of the date ESD announces the result in Student Portal. They may also inspect the questions and assessment criteria.

In practice This is exactly the line in your 'Grades Published' email template. Don't publish on a Friday afternoon if you can avoid it — the inspection clock starts immediately.
Art. 5.5Students & accommodations

Adjustments for disability / chronic illness

Students with a disability or chronic illness can request adapted exam conditions. Disability Support (DS) advises, the Board of Examiners decides. Adjustments must not alter difficulty or quality.

In practice This is the legal foundation of SPAR. When a SPAR edge case comes up that isn't in the planning overview, the decision-maker is the Board of Examiners — not ESD.
Art. 4.5Attendance

Attendance requirement

Modules require minimum 85% attendance (some 100%). Students who miss more than 15% but at most 30% with a valid reason may request an additional assignment to repair it. The Board of Examiners can exempt in exceptional cases.

In practice Source of the 70–85% additional-assignment rule in your grading rules. A student with a sufficient grade but failed attendance still fails the transcript. An attendance fail can NEVER come from missing the exam itself — only from missed tutorials/meetings.
Art. 5.3Exams

Twice per academic year

Each course examination is offered twice per AY: once during/after the course period (first sit), once during the rest of the year (resit). Periods are in the academic calendar.

In practice Legal basis for the resit period structure. The BoE can decide an exam may be taken at another date in exceptional cases.
Art. 5.4Exams

Registration for course examinations

ESD registers students for course examinations — both first sit and resit — based on their module registration. The student must meet attendance to actually sit.

In practice This is your ZPIQ_MASS_EXAM step for resits. First sit is automatic from the module registration.
Art. 5.11Process & appeals

Retention period for exams

Written exam questions, answers, and evaluated work must be kept (paper or digital) for 2 years after the result is determined. Capstones and theses: 7 years.

In practice When a CC asks 'can I throw these out?' — the answer is no, not for 2 years. Useful for the post-inspection cleanup question.
Art. 5.14Fraud & irregularities

Fraud

If the Board of Examiners determines a student has committed fraud, it takes appropriate measures. In serious cases the student can be deregistered from the programme by UM's Executive Board.

In practice Your irregularity report forms feed this process. You collect — the BoE decides. While a case is pending: withhold the grade (blank/TBD/NG) — a transcript entry cannot be undone. Provisional marks may still show in Canvas; that's acceptable, the transcript is not.
Art. 5.15Fraud & irregularities

Invalid exam

If irregularities make it impossible to accurately assess the student's knowledge, the Board of Examiners may declare the exam invalid for one student or a group.

In practice Power tool for cases like a fire alarm during an exam, system crash, or print error affecting many students. Escalate, don't decide alone.
03 / SUPPORTING

Good to know.

9 entries

Art. 4.4Students & accommodations

Module registration deadline

Students must register for modules by the stated deadline by submitting a registration form to ESD. Late registrations are not guaranteed; in exceptional circumstances some form may still be possible.

In practice Affects the student numbers you submit to AR at T−6 weeks. Late registrants can disturb room capacity.
Art. 5.1Exams

General — Rules of Procedure

Each module's course examination consists of at least two graded moments. The course manual states the ILOs and assessment criteria. The 'Rules of Procedure for (Course) Examinations at Maastricht University' describes operational rules.

In practice There is a separate UM-wide Rules of Procedure document referenced here — worth tracking down and linking. Most exam-day student rules originate from it.
Art. 5.6Exams

Oral exams

Oral exams are taken one student at a time. An examiner conducts them in the presence of another staff member. They take place in public unless the BoE or examiner decides otherwise.

In practice Rare for MSP, but if a CC arranges one, two staff need to be present. Not the standard exam-week workflow.
Art. 5.13Students & accommodations

Exemption

The Board of Examiners may grant exemption from a course examination if the student has previously passed a similar one elsewhere or has equivalent professional experience. Exemptions cover an entire module, not parts. Capstones/theses cannot be exempted.

In practice Affects which students appear on your grading sheets — exempted students should not be there.
Art. 6.1Graduation

Final examination / graduation

The Board of Examiners determines the date of the final examination. The graduation date is the last day of the month in which all obligations are satisfied. Graduation is automatic. Students may request postponement at least 1 month before the final assessment, in defined cases (dual degree, board year, etc.).

In practice When a student says they want to delay graduation, they need to ask the BoE — and the request must come in BEFORE the final assessment.
Art. 6.4Graduation

GPA calculation

GPA is the credit-weighted average of all numerical grades. Compensated failing modules are included. Credit-transfer credits are excluded.

In practice Useful when explaining final transcripts to students.
Art. 7.6Students & accommodations

Personal circumstances

Personal circumstances (illness, impairment, pregnancy, family circumstances, board activities) may be considered by the BoE. Must be reported to the BoE, with supporting documentation if requested. Students should also notify their study/academic advisor.

In practice Foundation for SPAR and individual exam adjustments. The student must report it formally — not just mention it in a hallway.
Art. 7.8Students & accommodations

Leave of absence

Students who want to take a leave of absence must consult Academic Advising and inform ESD before taking leave. They are reinstated to pre-leave status when they return.

In practice Sometimes returnees show up on registers unexpectedly. Cross-check leave status when something looks off.
Art. 8.6Process & appeals

Right of appeal

Students may appeal decisions of the Board of Examiners or examiners to the Board of Appeal for Examinations (CBE) via UM's Complaints Service Point, within 6 weeks of the decision.

In practice If a student disputes a grade or fraud finding, this is the formal channel. Six-week clock starts at the decision date.
Source: MSLAS Education and Examination Regulations 2025–2026 · For the authoritative text, consult the PDF.
